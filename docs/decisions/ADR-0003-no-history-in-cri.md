# ADR-0003 — Behavioral history must not enter the CRI computation

**Status:** Accepted

## Context

A recurring and intuitively reasonable proposal: since the system already
stores a per-student count of confirmed incidents, why not feed it into the CRI
so that students with prior confirmed incidents are scored as higher risk? Their
probability of cheating is, the argument goes, empirically higher.

The intuition is understandable — the data exists, and discarding it feels
wasteful. This ADR exists so that the reasoning against it does not have to be
reconstructed each time the idea resurfaces.

## Decision

Behavioral history — and any other per-student prior — must never influence CRI
computation. Not as a prior, not as an added term, not as a per-student weight,
not as a lowered alert threshold.

CRI is computed only from signals observed in the current session.

## Rationale

**It is prohibited by requirement.** NFR10 (Must-have, Legal & Ethical
Compliance) states that behavioral risk-profile enrichment must not be used to
alter the detection algorithm's sensitivity for any individual student. FR6's own
third clause qualifies history as attached "without altering the underlying CRI
computation itself."

**It creates a self-confirming feedback loop.** One confirmed incident raises
the baseline → the threshold is crossed more easily → more alerts are generated
for that student → more clips are reviewed → the probability of a second
confirmation rises → the baseline rises again. After a few sessions the student
sits in near-permanent alert state, not because their behaviour changed but
because the system classified them. The system ends up confirming its own
suspicions, and the evidence trail looks legitimate.

**It destroys the project's stated contribution.** Proposal §1.1 names "Proctor
Bias & Personal Familiarity" as the problem EyeQ addresses. Encoding priors into
the score reproduces exactly that bias, in a worse form: a human proctor's
prejudice can be questioned and held to account; a coefficient in a fusion
formula presents as objective.

If asked at defence what distinguishes EyeQ from a biased proctor, there must be
an answer. With history in the CRI, there is not one.

**Past conduct is not evidence of present conduct.** A student who cheated in one
exam is not thereby cheating in this one. The system measures observed behaviour.

## What to do with history instead

History is not discarded — it is used after the decision, never before or inside it.

1. **Post-session administrative reporting.** Aggregate confirmed incidents into
   a report for the exam control office. The consequential decision stays human
   and documented.
2. **Display ordering after generation.** Alerts of equal CRI may be ordered in
   the Controller's queue by history. The distinction is load-bearing: the alert
   was already generated by current behaviour; history only ordered the queue,
   and generated nothing. This approaches the boundary — if implemented, document
   it explicitly.
3. **A quality signal about the system, not the student.** A seat that
   repeatedly generates alerts which are all dismissed indicates a calibration or
   camera-angle problem at that seat. Here history evaluates EyeQ, not the student.

## Consequences

- `CRIEngine` takes no student identifier and no historical input. Its interface
  should stay that way; a change that adds one is the thing to reject.
- The CV engine has no access to the history store, structurally.
- Any future ML-based fusion must exclude per-student features from training.
